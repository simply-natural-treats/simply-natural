Straight Talking Dog Nutrition

We believe in cutting through the marketing fluff to tell you the truth about what goes into your dog's bowl. Browse our most frequently asked questions, myth-busters, and nutritional glossary below.

Honest Answers to Your Top Questions

Why don't you offer freeze-dried dog food?

Once seen as the ultimate superior choice, we followed this innovation with keen interest. However, the reality is that it is an extremely expensive and wasteful process, requiring significantly more raw meat to produce a small yield. As a result, it has largely been superseded by advancements in cold-pressed technology, which offers fantastic nutrition at a more accessible price point.

In fact, many manufacturers have quietly withdrawn their freeze-dried completes altogether. Be cautious of brands offering their standard kibble with a few freeze-dried treats mixed in, as these are often marketed as misleading hybrid products!

Why don't you offer raw food?

Actually, we do! However, we have observed a national trend showing that raw feeding is in decline. The pool of raw brands is shrinking, with many companies folding or being swallowed up by larger corporations. We believe this decline is largely due to incredible advancements in other manufacturing technologies, such as premium cold-pressed and luxury air-dried diets, which offer raw-quality nutrition with everyday convenience.

We do keep a modest stock of Nutriment’s Just Range in 500g trays for our loyal legacy customers, dogs who have known no different since they were pups. Their owners have become friends over the years, and this is exactly the kind of personal service we pride ourselves on.

Why don't you offer 'fresh' refrigerated food?

Quite simply, because it is a marketing fad. It offers no nutritional advantage over the premium food categories already available. It occupies a very grey area in the market, packaged and presented to appeal to owners who like the idea of raw feeding but do not actually want to feed raw. It is aimed squarely at the "toe-in-the-water" consumer browsing the supermarket aisles.

I can buy dog food and treats much cheaper elsewhere, why is yours more expensive?

Because everything on our shelves has been benchmarked against our incredibly high standards. We have been offering natural, grain-free, gluten-free, and hypoallergenic nutrition since 2019. We know that not all dogs can tolerate a grain-based diet, as they are simply not genetically engineered to process heavy carbohydrates. While some dogs are resilient and cope well on mass-produced diets, we are here for the ones who cannot.

It is also worth noting that confectionery giants like Mars and Nestlé dominate the vast majority of the pet food market. They own multiple brands, from budget supermarket bags to gourmet tins and even 'prescription' diets. True, independent, hypoallergenic ingredients cost more to source, but they pay dividends in your dog's long-term health and vitality.

Myth-Busters: Seeing Through the Spin

"Designed for Dogs"

Take a close look at the packaging. Most commercial pet food marketing is aimed entirely at the human buyer's appetite and emotions, not the dog's actual nutritional needs.

"Vet Approved"

This label sounds impressive until you realise the vet in question is usually employed directly by the company manufacturing the food.

"Natural Ingredients"

There is natural, and then there is natural. Many heavily processed chemicals technically derive from natural sources, but it absolutely does not mean they have a place in your dog’s diet.

"No Nasties"

This term actually means nothing. No food ingredients are legally classified as 'nasties'. It is simply a clever marketing trick to make a highly processed food sound wholesome.

"Cleans Your Dog's Teeth"

Standard kibble will not clean your dog's teeth. However, there are proper natural treats available that offer genuine abrasion, such as hooves or pizzles. We promise they help clean teeth, but we certainly cannot promise fresh breath!

"High protein makes dogs hyper"

We hear this constantly, and it simply is not true. If your dog is hyperactive, more exercise and mental enrichment is the answer. Starving your dog of the essential amino acids vital for growth, repair, and vitality will not calm them down.

The Simply Natural Glossary & Health Truths

Ingredients of Animal Origin

This legal term covers any and all parts of the animal. Nothing is wasted. If the label does not explicitly name the meat, it is not proper meat.

Open vs. Closed Menus

An 'Open Menu' tells you exactly what is in the food (e.g., "50% Freshly Prepared British Lamb").

A 'Closed Menu' uses vague umbrella terms like "Meat and animal derivatives". This allows manufacturers to constantly swap out the protein source from batch to batch based on whatever meat is cheapest that week.

Grain-Free vs. Gluten-Free

Not all grains contain gluten. For dogs with a gluten sensitivity or celiac disease, it is vital to differentiate between the two.

Note: If a label says "Free of gluten-containing ingredients", it means the recipe itself has no gluten, but it is manufactured on the same conveyor belts as foods that do. Therefore, cross-contamination cannot be avoided, and they cannot legally call it "Gluten-Free".

Dried Meat vs. Freshly Prepared

'Freshly Prepared' refers to fresh meat that leaves the exact same abattoir as human-grade meat, just on a different lorry heading to the pet food facility.

'Dried Meat' (often listed as meat meal) is a highly concentrated protein powder. While it can be a good source of protein, it is heavily processed and often of a much lower grade if the animal source is unnamed.

Hidden Allergens in Fish Oils

Many foods boast about added "Omega 3 Fish Oils". However, these are often derived from salmon or trout, which is completely unhelpful if your dog has a specific sensitivity to those fish.

The "Chicken and Rice" Advice

When a dog has an upset stomach, owners are often told to feed boiled chicken and white rice. Chicken is actually one of the most common canine intolerances we see, so if your dog is sensitive to it, this will not end well! Furthermore, while rice is gluten-free, it is still a grain. Canned pure pumpkin is a much more effective, holistic offering to settle a stomach.

"My dog just won't eat dog food!"

We hear this a lot! Simply put, if you treat your dog to a home-prepared meal of fresh chicken or fish every evening, they will hold out for it. Dogs are incredibly clever. They know that if they turn their nose up at their bowl long enough, you will eventually give in and cook them something special.

Epilepsy Triggers

We have learned a thing or two about canine epilepsy over the years. If your dog suffers from seizures, avoid feeding them rabbit, and keep well away from foods containing added glutamate (an excitatory neurotransmitter). Environmentally, you should also avoid burning synthetic scented candles or using essential oils in your home, as these can lower the seizure threshold.

Pancreatitis

Dogs managing pancreatitis require a highly digestible, strictly low-fat diet to prevent the pancreas from overworking. Premium semi-moist foods or specific hydrolysed proteins are often the most suitable and comfortable options.

Low Purine Diets

Purines are natural compounds found in food that break down into uric acid. Dogs with kidney issues, or specific breeds like Dalmatians prone to urate stones, require a low-purine diet. This means strictly avoiding offal (like liver and kidneys), certain seafood, and high-purine meats like venison.

'Prescription' Diets

Take a look at the ingredients list on a bag of expensive veterinary prescription food. More often than not, it is predominantly made up of heavy grains and very little actual meat.

Are Grain-Based Foods Bad?

Not necessarily. Dogs have evolved alongside humans, and many can tolerate grains perfectly well. However, a dog's ancestral diet would have consisted of very little grain and would have been made up entirely of quality protein and foraged botanicals. We prefer to feed them what they are built to thrive on.
